CHAPTER 07 · ONE-SAMPLE TESTS

Start with the big idea

GUIDED LESSON
01

What is hypothesis testing?

THEORY · WHY THIS METHOD EXISTS

A business often makes a claim about a population: average delivery time, average product weight, customer satisfaction, or defect rates. Measuring every member of that population is usually impractical.

Hypothesis testing gives us a disciplined way to use a sample to evaluate a population claim. We begin with a stated assumption (the null hypothesis) and ask whether the sample provides strong enough evidence against it.

✦
The central question

If the null hypothesis were true, would a sample result like this be unusual?

02

When should you use it?

RECOGNIZE THE PROBLEM

Look for a question that asks whether a population claim is supported by sample evidence. Typical wording includes “is there evidence that,” “has the average changed,” or “is the true mean different from.”

USE THIS METHODOne population parameter

You are evaluating a claim about one population mean or proportion.

CHECK FIRSTIdentify the conditions

Check the variable, sample design, distribution assumptions, and whether σ is known before selecting a test.

03

Build the hypotheses

TURN THE CLAIM INTO A TESTABLE QUESTION

The null hypothesis contains the equality. The alternative describes the evidence you are looking for.

Two-tailedH₁: μ ≠ μ₀Different from the claim
Right-tailedH₁: μ > μ₀Greater than the claim
Left-tailedH₁: μ < μ₀Less than the claim

Why does the null hypothesis include equality?

The test needs a specific reference model to calculate how surprising the observed sample would be. The null hypothesis supplies that reference point. The alternative describes departures from it. The data are assessed under the assumption that the null model is true; we do not calculate the probability that the null itself is true.

CHAPTER 7 · ONE-SAMPLE Z-TEST

Standardized distance from the claim

Measures how many standard errors separate the sample mean from the hypothesized population mean.

Use when: Testing a population mean with known population standard deviation, when test assumptions are satisfied.
z = (x̄ − μ₀)
────────────
(σ / √n)
FOUNDATION · STANDARD ERROR

Standard error of the mean

Describes the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample mean.

Why it matters: It measures sample-to-sample variability and forms the denominator of the Z statistic.
SE = σ / √n
